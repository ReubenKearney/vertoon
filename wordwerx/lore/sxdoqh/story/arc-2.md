# Arc 2 — Bandarpur & Insect Resilience

The team goes to Bandarpur to consult insect specialists because the nocturnal insects are becoming more resilient to resin smoke. Bandarpur was built on and in the water to keep distance from the insects, so as ocean levels recede that natural protection weakens, exposing ring harbours and underwater systems more directly to nocturnal hazard; at the same time tsunami threat and frequency rise. Migration inland looks attractive geographically, but conventional landward fallback is blocked by the declining resin yields established in Arc 1.

## The arc's twist

The Khanna Microfoundry Group is **not lying** about the danger — its warnings about tsunami risk and receding water are materially correct — but it uses that truth to advance a broader ideological and political goal: that humanity should expand beyond the narrow confines of the megacities. The team approaches Khanna's claims with suspicion, perhaps expecting to debunk them, and ends up corroborating the substance while rejecting the attached agenda. The arc also reveals more about what Echo is, deepens the insect mystery, pushes Indu further from infiltrator toward conflicted ally, and ends with another confirmed piece of destabilising truth — and with the team's movement giving Indu another traceable clue to Echo's location.

## Six-episode sketch

1. Arrival and consultation with insect specialists; first signs resin smoke is losing deterrent value. 2. Investigation of harbour exposure, underwater circulation, and why receding water changes the safety logic. 3. Suspicion that Khanna is manipulating the narrative, then evidence the risk models are sound. 4. Discovery that landward fallback is non-viable because of declining resin. 5. Public/political confrontation over who is moved, who is protected, and who profits. 6. The team leaves with another systemic data point — and Indu with another locational clue.
