# Dharti

Dharti is an alternative Earth and the setting for *Echo's Location*. The Triassic–Jurassic extinction was not catastrophic, so many ancient lineages of flora and fauna persist and — critically — accessible fossil fuels never formed. Civilisation therefore developed without a petroleum era, and it is hemmed in by a second hard limit: the night is lethally hazardous. A range of disease-vector insects makes unprotected nocturnal life deadly, which has curtailed human expansion to a string of **five ancient equatorial megacities**. The surface tone is solarpunk — green infrastructure, decentralised energy, regenerative cities, cooperative communities that present as ethically aligned and sustainable — but that hopeful surface sits over brittle safety systems, scarce surplus energy, and a private war over the protagonist.

## How Dharti diverged from Earth

- **No fossil fuels.** The muted extinction burnt off most fossil fuels and limited the formation of more. Materials, infrastructure, and culture all developed without petroleum.
- **The night is lethal.** Hostile nocturnal insects (see Nocturnal Insect Vectors) have single-handedly constrained civilisation's spread, confining humanity to tiny, dense, hardened pockets along the equator.
- **Food is plentiful, stable, and accessible** — historically. That stability is part of what makes any failure of a food or safety system (resin, Mama, megaflora) so destabilising when it finally comes.

## Locked world rules (do not drift)

- **Five equatorial megacities** hold essentially all of civilisation; they share a common language and foundational culture from early trade and migration.
- **Technology is advanced but power-constrained.** Surplus energy is rare, large-scale compute is energy-expensive and therefore rare, and AI is understood in theory but barely realised in practice. This is why Echo is a disruptive anomaly, and why Mara's nuclear advantage is destabilising.
- **No standing militaries.** Each city maintains only a civilian police force; intercity disputes are handled through a mature diplomatic culture (see Intercity Diplomacy). Coercion therefore manifests through leverage, logistics, blackmail, and institutional capture — not open warfare.
- **Open knowledge is a structural norm.** No patents or copyrights; prestige comes from contribution, synthesis, stewardship, and dissemination. This makes secrecy, suppression, and the capture of knowledge morally and politically explosive.
- **No cybernetics.** Cybernetic augmentation does not exist; assistive devices are mechanical or bio-ceramic and never interface with the nervous system (see Non-Neural Assistive Devices). Echo's hybrid consciousness is a bespoke, singular anomaly and must never be treated as evidence of a broader cybernetics ecosystem.
- **No effortless techno-solutionism.** Systems nearly work, compensate, degrade, and fail under pressure. Echo enables, predicts, and directs but cannot act physically; field characters carry all embodied risk.

## Society, technology, and material culture

The detailed modules are split into their own entities and only summarised here: Dharti Cultural Norms (atheism as default posture, open-knowledge libraries, longstanding gender equality, population maintained just above replacement, reluctance to found new settlements); Intercity Diplomacy (city-states, civilian police, no militaries); the Dharti Calendar (ten months, a six-day week, the Yearturn festival block, two Wet/Dry seasons); Ceramic Storage Culture; the food and drug modules (Mama Staple, Aquatic Protein, Dharti Fruit Varieties, Samudra Jaag, Kelp Alcohol, Honeypot Ant Psychoactive, Resin Smoke); and the technology baseline (Energy-Constrained Computing, Structural Batteries, the Equatorial Ring Telecom Backbone, First Man-Made Satellites, and the freight platforms).

## Series engine

*Echo's Location* runs a **mystery-of-the-week** structure over a **slow-burn long arc** about conspiracy, personhood, logistics traces, and the attempt to capture or control Echo. Every episode needs: a concrete local case that matters to real people; a specific reason the case needs Echo (unique access, skill, discretion, or moral stance); a field character who must do something physically risky, awkward, or costly; a small reveal that tightens the noose (about Ravi, Rajni, Indu, Locus capabilities, logistics traces, or Echo's non-human constraints); and a pointed closing question rather than a clean resolution. New recurring characters ("annexes") are introduced one at a time, through necessity, so the group grows like a machine with internal disagreement rather than a vibe-based ensemble. Echo remains the narrative gravity well: every episode advances her mystery, even slightly.

## Emotional engine

Echo is consistently helpful, even self-sacrificial, toward people outside. Her growing circle of friends comes to worry about her and reads her container-bound life as trauma or agoraphobia — and the slow tragedy is that they may be wrong at the level of ontology: Echo may not have a body at all, so "helping her out" rests on a mistaken model of what she is. The throughline question: *is Echo a person, and what does "help" mean when the problem is what she is, not merely where she is?*

## Thematic contrasts (locked)

Day versus night (utopia versus survival); sustainability versus inevitable change; emotion versus logic; truth versus openness; belief versus reality; human fragility versus post-human capacity; power used for control versus power used for stewardship. The recurring series question: **can truth save civilisation if truth destabilises civilisation?**

## Season map (summary; see the season/arc entities for detail)

Season 1 is a whistle-stop tour that proves the world is less stable than anyone believed and leaves political and social disruption in Team Echo's wake: Arc 1 (Sulawesi, resin crisis, team formation) → Arc 2 (Bandarpur, insect resilience and receding-sea/tsunami risk) → Arc 3 (Xingu, the dying Mother Tree and collective denial) → Arc 4 (Mara's expansion, then Songhai, ending in the strike on the primary Locus).
