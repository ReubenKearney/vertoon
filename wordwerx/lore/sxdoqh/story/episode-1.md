# Episode 1 — Wrong Place, Right Voice

Status: locked. Function: introduce Dharti's nocturnal threat, prove Echo's constraint, establish Neelai as POV, and bring Wulan in as the first civic anchor. Primary POV: Neelai only. Setting: Sulawesi at dusk, transitioning into lockdown and night.

## Locked narrative progression

Neelai speaks by phone with her editor in Mara while moving through Sulawesi at dusk, which organically introduces the lamp-and-sensor safety system, resin dependence, and the city's assumption of routine security. The environment shifts from warm, ordinary daytime life into visible lockdown logic as the light fades. Following a lead, Neelai enters a lamp outpost / lantern hub and inspects records, discovering logbook evidence that increasing concentrations are being used in the repellent fuel, and photographs it. A mosquito lands on her inside a supposedly protected office — the instant a bureaucratic anomaly becomes embodied danger. She flees into access tunnels; Echo calls unexpectedly and guides her, but must show at least one hesitation, recalculation, misread, or partial failure so her limitation is tested, not merely stated. At the final barrier she reaches a locked hatch Echo cannot open; Wulan opens it manually from the far side, uses a portable repellent device (fire-extinguisher in feel) to drench her, pulls her through, and seals the hatch. The episode ends with Neelai alive but shaken, Echo disconnected, Wulan terse and practical, and the systemic implications unresolved.

## Scene and tone rules

Echo is voice-only in the debut (no full visual avatar). Wulan must be seeded before the rescue so it reads as systemic presence, not coincidence. Neelai's own investigative choice creates the danger. The threat should feel biological and civilisational, not monster-of-the-week.
