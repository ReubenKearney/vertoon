# Arc 3 — Xingu & the Mother Tree

The team travels to Xingu because Echo identifies Kaie as someone they need to find. Kaie is a street-urchin survivalist whose parents were tied to buried knowledge about the Mother Tree's decline. The arc's mystery is deliberately different from Arc 1 or 2: not a hidden technical fact but a **visible, socially denied reality**. The populace has become so reliant on the Mother Tree that obvious signs of failing health are treated as cyclical, temporary, or impolite to name — the emotional logic of the Newfoundland cod collapse, where communities could see depletion but clung to the belief that the system had always recovered before. Elders insist the tree has been through bad periods and always recovered; this is a seasonal phase; the city has always endured.

## Kaie's reckoning

Kaie begins believing there was a murderous cover-up around their parents, then discovers a harder, sadder truth: their parents likely died in an accident caused by the tree's declining health rather than an assassination conspiracy. The catharsis is Kaie shifting from blame to acceptance and choosing to help others face reality.

## Locked motifs and sketch

Arborists climb higher and farther to find healthy growth (a practical sign conditions are worsening); a branch failure or similar undeniable event punctures the protective social story; the truth going public leaves another wave of civic turmoil in the team's wake. Six-episode sketch: 1. Arrival and first contact with Kaie amid dismissed signs of decline. 2. Kaie frames the parents' fate as deliberate suppression. 3. The team uncovers civic secrecy, paternalistic silence, and social denial rather than a clean villain. 4. More evidence surfaces in public space as nocturnal risk rises. 5. Kaie learns the loss was ecological failure, not murder. 6. The dying of the Mother Tree gets out, destabilising Xingu and pushing the season toward its finale.
