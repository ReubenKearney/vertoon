# Crown Lantern Lightwell System

Mara's citywide light-and-air system, centred on the Crown Lantern — a singular summit megastructure that reads as the city's iconic "cap" above Mt Kenya and continues as a structural spine into the interior volumes.

## Light sourcing, conditioning, and distribution

All illumination delivered through the city's lightwell network originates at the Crown Lantern (the **singular-origin rule**). Daylight is harvested at the summit and conditioned before distribution: infrared is separated and handled at the top so solar heat is not dumped into living volumes, and ultraviolet is filtered to safe trace levels. In low sun and at night the Crown Lantern injects crown-generated illumination into the same network for sky-cycle continuity. Distribution uses sealed guided-light infrastructure (not open shafts): 8 independently isolatable Light Spines → Ring Galleries → District Lightwells → capillary runs into streets, corridors, and courtyards. An **equity rule** mandates diffusion surfaces in inhabited zones to prevent glare and "premium light" segregation, and a citywide circadian programme runs a consistent sky cycle (dawn ramp, midday plateau, dusk ramp, night safety mode).

## Air and resilience

The Crown Lantern also hosts the primary ventilation headworks — a semi-closed atmospheric loop with controlled make-up air, high-capacity particulate filtration and biological screening, heat recovery, CO₂ management and oxygen supplementation, and humidity control. The city is divided into pressure and fire zones with isolation gates at ring galleries and district interfaces, with storm, fire, and contamination safety modes. The Crown Lantern is singular but built with redundant subsystems so maintenance or faults never collapse citywide function. It is kept by the Lanternwrights.
