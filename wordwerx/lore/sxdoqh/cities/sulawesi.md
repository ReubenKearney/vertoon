# Sulawesi

Sulawesi is one of Dharti's five megacities and, per the master canon, its largest, located on Sulawesi (Indonesia). It is the **only Dharti megacity that is entirely on land**, because the lamp-based nocturnal insect management system makes normal high density viable despite the night threat. Where the other cities are shaped by water, canopy, mountain, or shoal, Sulawesi can be planned and built as a "traditional" megacity more akin to an Earth megacity such as Jakarta. Its primary power is geothermal. It is the opening city of the series and the site of the first crisis.

## The lamp system and the Guild

Public insect-repellent infrastructure is delivered through extensive **wick lamp systems** arrayed throughout streets and outside homes (see Wick Lamp Insect-Management Network). Their primary purpose is not illumination: they minimise light pollution and direct flame-light downward to avoid attracting insects from a distance. The fuel is resin-based. The lamps are colloquially called **DALL·E** for their appearance.

The lamps are kept by the ancient **Guild of Lamplighters**, funded and administered separately from government to ensure continuity and neutrality, and funded at neighbourhood level through local collection stations or integrated collection mechanisms on each lamp. Stealing from lamps is an extreme social taboo with severe penalties, and effectively never occurs. Lamplighters live frugally, are sworn to political neutrality, walk every street in their territory on nightly rounds, and hold a clergy-adjacent civic role grounded in life-saving service rather than theology. (Full detail in the Guild of Lamplighters entry.)

## The resin crisis (Arc 1)

The entire scale and safety model of Sulawesi rests on the potency, availability, and reliability of resin — a reliability that has never failed in recorded history. Arc 1 turns on the discovery that resin yield is declining: refinement is compensating by increasing concentration, but the raw biological input is objectively weakening and has been for years. The cause currently locked at season-plan level is a **natural, irreversible biological evolution** — the resin is gradually becoming less potent and there is no fix; refinement only buys time. The civilisation-level dilemma is that Sulawesi may not be permanently sustainable at its current scale: perimeter contraction, neighbourhood prioritisation, and the politics of who gets protected first all become unavoidable, which makes Wulan's triage authority morally explosive.
