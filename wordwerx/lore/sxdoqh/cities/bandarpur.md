# Bandarpur

Bandarpur is a single **megacity-state** spanning the real-world Adam's Bridge (Rama Setu) shoal chain between Pamban Island (India) and Mannar Island (Sri Lanka). It is organised as two extreme-density land endcaps anchoring a segmented inhabited spine along the shoals, with twelve circular harbour boroughs set off the spine. The system is designed to read clearly on a top-down map, to be architecturally distinctive through ring-vestibule urbanism, and to be operationally credible through night-hardening and protected underwater layers. Its primary power is tidal/hydro, and it is Dharti's major port and logistics hub.

## Governance and identity

Bandarpur operates as one megacity-state entity with unified administration, infrastructure planning, and a single census frame across the endcaps, spine, and harbour boroughs.

## Geography baseline

The map basis is the current real geography of Adam's Bridge (Rama Setu), including the shoal chain between Dhanushkodi (Pamban Island) and Talaimannar (Mannar Island), with the Palk Strait to the north and the Gulf of Mannar to the south.

## Underwater build share

About **25%** of total citywide floor area is below the waterline. In the harbour boroughs, **30%** of borough floor area is below the waterline; of that underwater floor area, **60% is habitable** and **40%** is utilities, freight, ballast, and water systems. The underwater layer is core to both the city's visual identity and its night-safe operations, using compartmentalisation, protected circulation, and light-well systems.

## Spatial layout

- **West endcap (India side):** extreme-density land city with one residential mega tower.
- **East endcap (Sri Lanka side):** extreme-density land city with one residential mega tower.
- **Adam's Bridge spine:** a segmented inhabited chain aligned to the shoals, with gaps over open water.
- **Off-spine harbours:** 12 circular harbour boroughs, alternating north and south of the spine.

### Tidal throats (navigation and energy gates)

- **Jaya Gate** — primary tidal throat and navigation control point.
- **Vijaya Gate** — secondary tidal throat and navigation control point.

Both gates appear on the map as deep navigation channels crossing the shoal chain, paired with gateworks infrastructure and managed shipping corridors.

## Harbour borough system

**Standard harbour anatomy:** an inhabited outer breakwater ring (powerworks, sluice grilles, maintenance gantries, controlled access); a calmer inner-lagoon commons (civic decks, markets, water-transport lanes); 2–4 radial causeway/service spokes connecting to the spine (not rail); and a continuous below-water hull layer (the 30% underwater rule).

**Size classes:** S (6–7 km diameter, ~0.9 M each, 3 total); M (8–9 km, ~1.6 M each, 3 total); L (10–11 km, ~2.2 M each, 3 total); XL (12–14 km, ~3.0 M each, 3 total).

**Hero harbours (story focus):** one per size class — Muthu Mandala (S), Kaveri Bazaar Mandala (M), Varuna Sabha Mandala (L), and Jaya Gateworks Mandala (XL).

## Underwater urbanism

Within harbour boroughs the underwater layer divides into **Hull Districts** (habitable: compact residential/civic blocks around light wells and sealed transfer lobbies), **Vault Corridors** (protected below-water movement and commerce corridors that stay operational in rough weather and night protocols), and **Plant & Ballast** (non-habitable: water treatment, waste processing, freight handling, buoyancy control, maintenance access).

## Transport

Bandarpur uses the Hovercraft-Maglev Freight System: maglev infrastructure is restricted to land and island segments, and across open-water gaps vehicles transition to hovercraft operation along designated hover corridors. Network layers comprise land metros and endcap interchanges; shoal/island maglev guideways; dashed-route marine hover corridors; and harbour access causeways/spokes (no rail symbols across water). Operational constraints: stations and major public buildings use nested ring vestibules that seal progressively as hazard rises; hover legs slow or suspend in rough seas, with protected submerged approaches preferred near gateworks; freight concentrates in protected night windows, especially in XL gateworks harbours.

## Architectural identity

Recurring motifs: **the Rings** (ring vestibules and circular thresholds across stations, markets, and civic buildings); **the Throats** (heavy, infrastructurally loud gateworks with turbine galleries and controlled navigation apertures); and **the Waterline Band** (consistent below-water glazing and intake grilles marking the underwater hull). Most districts are low-rise (2–10 storeys) with vertical punctuation at interchanges. Each endcap holds one **mega tower** — a stepped arcology with multiple cores, stacked neighbourhood plates, and sealed transfer lobbies integrated with transit.

## Narrative role (Arc 2)

Bandarpur is the focus of Arc 2. The team comes to consult insect specialists because the nocturnal insects are becoming more resilient to resin smoke; meanwhile the city was built on and in the water to keep distance from the insects, so as ocean levels recede that natural protection weakens and tsunami risk rises. The arc's twist is that the Khanna Microfoundry Group is **not lying** about the danger — its warnings are materially correct — but it uses that truth to push an expansionist agenda. Movement through Bandarpur also gives Indu another traceable clue to Echo's location. (See `arc-2`.)
