# Xingu

Xingu is one of Dharti's five equatorial megacities, located in the Amazon Basin, with wind as its primary power. The city is built around a single megaflora organism: 200 hub trees (giant orange ginkgo-like canopy trees, 100–200 m tall) all branches of one organism connected to a central **Mother Tree** roughly 400 m tall and 100 m in diameter. The megaflora acts as both structural foundation — raising the city above the Amazon canopy, where people live to be safe from insects — and as a natural repellent to Dharti's hazardous nocturnal insects. Xingu targets a population of 50 million across a footprint listed as 2,000 square kilometres (unit to be confirmed). Full biological detail is in the Xingu Megaflora & Mother Tree entry.

## Architecture

The built form is integrated with the megaflora but clearly engineered, not "grown magic." It uses bio-ceramic composites, advanced plant-derived fibres, lightweight high-strength alloys, modular platforms, tensile structures, and disciplined mechanical interfaces, and deliberately avoids mystical or "wood-elf" ornamentation in favour of functional, maintainable, industrial-biophilic design. The mass-transit system uses trunk loops and ring lines at a few standard canopy levels, radial links to a central 500 m hub, short spurs into each hub's station terraces, and bypass sections at major hubs for express services.

## The dying Mother Tree (Arc 3)

The megaflora's growth and healing capacity have slowed for years. The Mother Tree is slowly dying of old age — not disease, mismanagement, or sabotage — and there is no recovery solution; inevitably the Mother Tree and all connected hubs will die and rot away. A small, secret core group of arborists and government officials conceals this from the public. As the megaflora fails, Xingu loses both its structural infrastructure and its insect protection, exposing it to the civilisation-wide limiting threat. Arc 3's distinctive mystery is **social denial** rather than a hidden fact: the populace treats visible thinning, slowed healing, and failed regrowth as cyclical, temporary, or impolite to name (the emotional logic of the Newfoundland cod collapse — "the tree has recovered before"). Kaie, a Xingu street-urchin survivalist whose parents were tied to the suppressed truth, is the arc's emotional core.
