Runs a night crew responsible for a "light perimeter" zone: the streets, alleys,
footbridges, and edges of habitation where the insects test defences. Their work
begins before dusk, peaks in the first two hours of full dark, and becomes
emergency triage until dawn. They do not just fix lamps — they decide which
blocks get saved first when resources are short. That is a moral and political
job.

**Signature skill — triage and routing under panic.** When things go wrong Wulan
narrows the failure to a single cause (fuel, wick, wind, sabotage, swarm
pressure, human error), reroutes people and light to "seal the wound", and buys
time, because time is what keeps people alive.

**Core wound.** They lost someone to a preventable night failure — bureaucratically
specific, not a generic tragedy: a requisition delayed, a junior too proud to
call for help, an inspector who signed off substandard oil. Every lamp is now a
personal debt, which makes their empathy toward Echo believable.

**Taboo line.** They will not lie to the neighbourhood about risk. They can bend
rules, steal supplies, and break chain-of-command, but they will not tell people
"you're safe" if they are not — which creates direct conflict when Echo wants
secrecy.

At first Wulan reads Echo as a brilliant shut-in terrified of leaving the
container — someone to help, because helping is what they do. Over time they
realise the tragedy is not agoraphobia but ontology. Their arc: bystander → the
one who opens the hatch.
